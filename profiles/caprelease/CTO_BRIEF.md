# CapRelease — CTO discussion brief

**Edward Carvajal Martinez · Senior QA Engineer / QA Lead**

## My proposition

“I would own quality across the delivery process and remain hands-on. I start by understanding your product, people, integrations and actual release decisions. I use AI to accelerate structured analysis, coverage review, query/test proposals and reporting, while I remain accountable for the judgment and evidence. My aim is to make important failure scenarios visible earlier, verify the controls that protect the process and give the team a clear release recommendation.”

## From the first day

I would align on the immediate problem, follow one critical workflow, review one current change and one available production escape, and pair with the existing tester. The day closes with a reviewed or explicitly provisional map of facts, questions, risks, blockers and the next action. With no system access, source analysis and coverage design still progress; execution stays Not Run.

## Why this fits CapRelease

The four data signals mean quality must include what the data represents, how it changes and where it is consumed. I would personally connect web/API behavior to SQL Server/Azure SQL evidence and investigate integration retries, throttling, partial data and recovery using confirmed contracts. I would verify cross-service assumptions between the .NET APIs and Python services, without inventing their architecture or business rules.

Automation would target stable, important protections at the right layer. A suite becomes release evidence only after actual execution and verified pipeline enforcement. For non-functional needs, I would agree measurable expectations and coordinate appropriate performance, resilience, accessibility and security verification. I would confirm applicable regulatory expectations with the responsible owner.

## The evidence I would report

| Executive question | Indicator / evidence |
|---|---|
| Which identified risks have working protections? | Verified risk mitigation, with required controls and current evidence |
| What essential exposure remains? | Unresolved critical risks, consequence, owner and next action |
| Can we trust the critical process? | Critical process assurance plus source-to-decision and data-rule diagnostics |
| Are we resolving issues early? | Shift-left readiness and preventive findings with actual chronology |
| Are integrations and required quality attributes verified? | Failure-mode/contract coverage and non-functional assurance |
| Is the improvement sustainable? | Enforced automation, mentoring evidence, feedback timing and mature production-escape cohorts |

Every percentage has a defined population and source. Unknowns remain Not measured. Costs, savings and ROI are outside this QA reporting profile. I do not promise an incident-reduction percentage before establishing a baseline.

## Leadership and challenge

I would set proportionate standards, prioritize team capacity and help the tester develop independent risk-based judgment. I would raise significant concerns proactively in writing and meetings. When evidence is insufficient, I would explain the process consequence, recommend the appropriate hold/No-Go or Pending assessment and identify the action needed. The authorized release owner records the actual release decision.

## Inspectable portfolio achievement

I have built the cross-sector method, 35 reusable skill entry points, traceability templates and an evidence-driven reporting workflow. These are portfolio deliverables, not claimed CapRelease results. The practical demonstration should use one real sanitized item and show the chain from source to AI proposal, accountable correction, actual verification and decision.
